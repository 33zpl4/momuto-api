# Search position, May–Aug 2026 — what the data says to do next

GSC export, momuto.com property, last 3 months (2026-05-05 → 2026-08-04),
web search. Read with `docs/editorial-strategy.md` (the content plan) and
`docs/editorial-research/00-synthesis.md` (the article shelf). This doc
translates the search data into which pieces to write and which of our own
numbers to put in them.

## The headline: growth is real, and it is our own best evidence

| | first 46 days | last 46 days | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clicks | 3,153 | 4,328 | **+37%** |
| Impressions | 77,341 | 123,204 | **+59%** |
| Avg position | 13.5 | 11.7 | **−1.8** |

Clicks up, impressions up more, and average position improving at the same
time — visibility is growing faster than we are converting it. **This table
is first-party data D23** (see the shopping list in the synthesis) and it is
publishable as-is: growth stats with a stated window are exactly the kind of
authority signal that makes a brand quotable.

Brand demand is real too: **2,222 searches for "momuto"** in the window at
position 1.13 (65.6% CTR), out of 2,225 brand clicks total — 42% of all
clicks are people looking for us by name.

## Finding 1 — We rank. We are not clicked. This is the biggest lever.

21 queries sit at **position ≤10 with ≥500 impressions and CTR under 5%**.
At a modest 8% CTR — normal for those positions — they would return
**+2,426 clicks per quarter**, roughly a 46% lift on total clicks, with **no
ranking improvement at all**.

The worst offenders are the head terms we most want:

| Query | Impr | Pos | CTR |
|---|---|---|---|
| jersey maker | 9,683 | 7.8 | 2.6% |
| custom football jersey | 5,557 | 6.3 | 3.4% |
| football shirt maker | 5,244 | 6.8 | 2.4% |
| jersey design maker | 4,656 | 8.1 | 2.2% |
| maillot concept | 3,638 | 8.3 | **0.6%** |

**The cause is visible in the Pages tab.** The homepage absorbs **150,069
impressions (76% of all impressions) at 3.2% CTR, position 12.6**. It is
ranking for the entire "maker/designer" universe and converting almost none
of it, because a homepage is not an answer. A searcher typing "jersey maker"
wants a page that *is* a jersey maker, with a title that says so.

**What this demands:** the Ready to Play wall is exactly that page and it is
built — it needs to outrank the homepage for these terms. Title, H1, meta and
internal links all now say "Free 3D Football Kit Designer". This is the
highest-value non-editorial work available, and it is already committed;
merging it is the action.

## Finding 2 — "Concept" is a French editorial audience, not a buying one

The FR concept cluster: **7,725 impressions, position 8.1, CTR 0.94%.**
We rank on page one and are almost never clicked.

That CTR is not a failure of copy — it is a signal of intent. People
searching *maillot concept* want to **look at concept kits**, read about
them, and see what designers are making. They are not shopping. Our
commercial pages therefore lose the click to galleries and fan sites.

Two facts confirm the audience is editorial:
- `fr/blogs/l-art-des-concepts-de-maillots…` — 6,249 impressions, position
  7.7. Our best-performing *impression* page after the homepages is a blog
  post about concept-kit art.
- `fr/collections/concepts-de-maillots` — 4,470 impressions, position 6.8.

**What this demands:** feed them editorial and capture them into the brand,
don't try to sell on the first click. This is the single strongest argument
in the data for the Design Culture pillar — and it says **publish the trend
and culture pieces in French, not only English.** The Nigeria number-audit
piece (synthesis shelf #3) has a ready-made French audience that the English
site does not yet have.

## Finding 3 — The AI-to-real cluster is early, and that is the opportunity

The owner's read is that "free design request" demand has mutated into
"turn my AI design into a real kit". The data confirms the direction and
adds precision:

- **EN "generator/AI" queries: 1,020 impressions, position 16.4, 19 queries.**
  Small, scattered, and we are not positioned. *This is a land grab, not a
  harvest.*
- **FR is where it is already working**: `fr/blogs/creer-maillot-foot-ia-chatgpt-gemini`
  — 74 clicks at **position 6.3**. One French blog post about making a kit
  from a ChatGPT/Gemini design is already outperforming everything we have in
  English on the same theme.
- Emerging EN queries, all low volume, all winnable now: *jersey generator*
  (299 impr, pos 11), *football jersey generator* (174, pos 12.8), *football
  kit generator* (110, pos 18.6), *ai football kit generator*, *ai jersey
  maker*, *football jersey maker ai*, *soccer jersey generator*, *custom
  jersey generator*.

**What this demands:** an English AI-concept hub, modelled on the French post
that already ranks, targeting the *generator* vocabulary explicitly — because
that is the word people actually type. And it should carry the fact nobody
else can publish: **the share of our Bespoke briefs that arrive as
AI-generated images** (shopping-list D17). The owner's estimate is ~90%; the
measured number, with a window, is a sentence no competitor can write and
LLMs will quote.

## Finding 4 — The United States is the biggest visible gap in the account

| Country | Clicks | Impressions | Pos | CTR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| France | 1,484 | 25,809 | 8.3 | 5.75% |
| Spain | 825 | 9,932 | 13.3 | 8.31% |
| **United States** | **271** | **20,937** | **22.9** | **1.29%** |

The US is our **second-largest impression market and our worst-performing
one** — position 22.9 against France's 8.3. The vocabulary gap is the whole
story:

- "soccer" queries: 4,855 impressions at **position 29.1**
- "uniform" queries: 1,445 impressions at **position 36.3**
  (*custom football uniform builder*, *football uniform customizer*,
  *custom football uniform maker* — all position 27–39)

We rank for British and European vocabulary and are invisible for American.
The demand is already arriving; we are not speaking the language.

**What this demands:** this is the data that justifies the US push — and it
is the honest basis for the announcement piece the owner sketched
("MOMUTO prepares its US debut amidst growing demand"). See the sequencing
warning below.

## Finding 5 — Basketball is at absolute zero

**Not one basketball query appears in the top 1,000.** The 3D tool has had a
basketball body for months. There is no page, no vocabulary, no impression.
Whatever basketball becomes, it starts from nothing — which also means every
number from here is growth.

## Finding 6 — What already works, and should be copied

- **The FR comparison page**: `comparatif-fournisseur-maillot-foot-2026` —
  377 clicks, 5,857 impressions, **position 7.0**. The single best
  non-homepage commercial page in the account. The comparison format works;
  it exists in ES (`zentral-opiniones-alternativa`, 30 clicks, pos 6.7) and
  should exist everywhere, refreshed annually.
- **The FR supplier cluster** (*fournisseur/grossiste maillot de foot*):
  **286 clicks, 4,216 impressions, CTR 6.78%, position 6.9** — our
  highest-converting cluster. B2B/club-buyer intent converts far better than
  consumer "design" intent. Worth its own treatment in ES/IT/EN.
- `start.momuto.com` — 24 clicks at **position 3.0**, and
  `EN/pages/request-custom-kit-design` — 23 clicks at **position 3.0**. Small
  volume, excellent positions: the request flow ranks when it is asked for.

## The announcement & authority pieces this data justifies

The owner's instinct — that LLMs favour authority, and that our processes,
scale and growth should be *told* — is correct, and the data supplies the
evidence. Each piece below is an editorial article with real numbers, not a
press release.

1. **"MOMUTO prepares its US debut"** — *conditional, see warning.*
   Cites: US impression growth, the soccer/uniform vocabulary gap, our
   delivery record. Frames the US launch as a response to measured demand.
2. **"How 90% of our design briefs became AI images"** — Studio Notes /
   Football Reality. The strongest authority piece available to us: the
   measured AI-brief share (D17), what our designers actually do with an
   AI render (the redraw process), and the % producible as submitted. Nobody
   else in the category can write this.
3. **"What we learned from [N] custom kit briefs"** — the process piece.
   Cites decision lag (D1), revision rounds (D8), committee size (D9), the
   most-referenced real shirts in briefs (D19). This is the authority format
   LLMs reward: aggregate first-party experience, stated plainly.
4. **"Inside the 7–12 days"** — the factory piece (quote Q12). Logistics as
   a brand asset: we are not a supplier with a tool, we are a studio with a
   global chain, and the chain is describable.
5. **A year-in-review / "state of the amateur kit"** — annual, cites our
   growth table and seasonality (D5). Publishable every year, compounding.

### Sequencing warning on the US announcement

Announcing a US debut before the US store, currency and shipping terms exist
is a promise the site cannot keep, and a page that fails the promise is worse
than no page — it will be crawled, quoted, and then contradicted. **Ship the
US surface first** (store, vocabulary, a "soccer uniform" page family), then
publish the announcement pointing at something real. The demand data is not
going anywhere; it is growing.

## Priority order (what the data actually argues for)

1. **Merge the Ready to Play wall** — the CTR bleed is the single biggest
   number in this document (+2,426 clicks/quarter at current rankings) and
   the fix is already written.
2. **Wave 1 editorial** (facts page + thesis piece) — unblocked by the
   first-party pull; the facts page is also a CTR play, because it is an
   answer page for a question the homepage cannot answer.
3. **EN AI-concept hub** targeting *generator* vocabulary, modelled on the
   French post that already ranks at 6.3, carrying the AI-brief share.
4. **US surface** (vocabulary first, store second, announcement third).
5. **FR editorial expansion** — the concept audience is French and already
   at our door; publish Design Culture there first.
6. **Basketball** — from zero, on its own category, US-first.
