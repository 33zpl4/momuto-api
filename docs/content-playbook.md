# Momuto content playbook — tensions, awareness levels, and the CTA ladder

How we apply persuasion psychology (3 tensions / 5 levers / Schwartz awareness
levels) to every piece of Momuto content: blogs, product pages, RTP pages,
configurator copy, share flows. Written to be read by humans AND by the agent
generating content — treat it as the standing instruction.

## 1. Who we're talking to (the tension map)

The buyer is almost never "a football player". It's **the organizer** — the
captain, club manager, peña founder, corporate event planner, futsal team's
"guy who sorts things" — ordering for 10–20 other people, with their name on
the outcome.

| Tension | How it shows up for the kit organizer |
|---|---|
| **Survival** (fear/problem) | Season/tournament deadline. Budget approval. Ordering wrong sizes for 15 people. Kits arriving late or looking cheap — *and everyone knowing it was your job*. |
| **Identity** (tribe/status) | The kit IS the tribe: club colours, crest, neighbourhood, "we look like a real team, not a pub side". The organizer's status inside the group rides on the result. |
| **Progress** (transformation) | Match day: the moment the team walks out in a kit *they designed*. Pride, permanence ("this season's shirt"), the photo everyone posts. |

Identity is our dominant lever — stronger for us than for almost any other
product category. Survival (deadlines/sizing/being blamed) is the opener;
progress (match-day image) is the closer.

## 2. Awareness levels → content types

Assign every piece a level BEFORE writing. The structure follows from it.

| Level | Reader state | Our content | Job of the piece |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Doesn't know custom kits are attainable | Social posts, top-funnel blog | Name the problem ("your team looks like five different teams") |
| Problem aware | Knows the kit situation is bad, not how to fix | SEO blogs (deadlines, funding, sizing, club onboarding) | Name the solution category + our angle |
| Solution aware | Comparing custom-kit vendors | RTP/collection pages, "how it works", comparison blogs | Differentiate: 3D design tool, 24h preview, 1 revision, 20–25 day production |
| Product aware | Knows Momuto, hasn't ordered | Product pages, testimonials, FAQ blocks | Proof + objection handling (min qty, price, sizing, delivery) |
| Most aware | Designed something, didn't order | Share/save flows, retargeting, drafts email | The nudge: their design already exists — finish it |

Mismatch is the classic failure: writing feature-first (product-aware copy)
for a problem-aware blog reader, or problem-naming for someone already in the
configurator.

## 3. The five levers, in Momuto voice

**1. Name the threat — open every blog with the problem, never with us.**
- ❌ "At Momuto we offer high-quality custom football kits…"
- ✅ "The season starts in six weeks. If the kit order isn't in this month,
  your team plays September in last year's shirts."

**2. Mirror the identity — "if you're…" targeting the organizer role.**
- "If you're the one who got volunteered to sort the kits this year…"
- "If your five-a-side team is one bad WhatsApp poll away from playing in
  mismatched shirts…"

**3. Exclude (sparingly, honestly) — draw the line that defines the tribe.**
- "This isn't for buying eleven plain shirts off a marketplace. It's for teams
  that want their own design — crest, colours, names, numbers."
- Never fake-exclude; our real lines are: min quantity 10, teams not
  individuals, custom design not blank stock.

**4. Paint the transformation — simulate match day, specifically.**
- "Three weeks from now the box arrives. Twenty shirts, your colours, your
  crest, a name and number on every back. The team group chat goes quiet, then
  explodes."
- On pages: renders/photos do this visually — a design on a body, not a flat
  template. (The 3D tool exists precisely to let the buyer FEEL ownership
  before paying.)

**5. Give the first step — the configurator IS the first step. Always.**
- Every piece ends at the smallest possible action: "Open the 3D designer and
  try your club's colours — two minutes, nothing to sign up for."
- Never end at "contact us" or a bare product link when a design action is
  possible. Once someone builds *their* kit, the Zeigarnik effect works for
  us: an unfinished design is an open loop that wants closing (Save/Share
  deepen it — a shared draft recruits the whole tribe into the loop).

## 4. Blog recipe (apply top to bottom)

1. Pick the awareness level and the dominant tension for the topic.
2. First sentence names the threat (survival) or mirrors the role (identity).
   No throat-clearing, no "in this article".
3. Body: problem → cost of inaction → solution category → our specific
   mechanism (3D tool, 24h preview, 1 revision, production window) → proof.
4. One transformation passage (match-day simulation) before the CTA.
5. CTA = configurator deep link with the smallest step framed in time
   ("2 minutes"). Secondary CTA at most one (RTP collection).
6. Same structure in EN/ES/FR — tension translates, examples localize
   (peñas, futsal ligas locales, corporate five-a-side).

## 5. How this applies to the configurator work (current task)

- **The wizard's colour step is our strongest psychological moment** — picking
  team colours is pure identity. Which is why the current bug (chosen colours
  not visibly applied to the design that opens) is a *commercial* defect, not
  a cosmetic one: it breaks the promise at peak attention. Fix ranks above
  cosmetics.
- **Welcome copy** is cheerleading without tension ("Time to craft your custom
  football kit…"). It should mirror + promise: e.g. "Your club's colours. Your
  design. Delivered in 25 days." — and the empty panel should paint the
  transformation (a rendered kit), or the step should go.
- **Design names carry identity.** Vendor leftovers (SASH, FENCE, GRANITE) say
  nothing; RTP naming (The Apex, The Fracture, The Legacy) is the right
  instinct. Templates onboarded via the converter get named, storied entries —
  the menu is a shop window, not a file list.
- **Save/Share is the tribe lever.** A shared draft = one organizer recruiting
  the whole team into the open loop. Share copy should be written as a message
  to teammates ("This is next season's shirt. Object now or wear it."), not a
  generic "check out my design".
- **Product pages** (custom products from validated designs) are product-aware
  surfaces: proof, delivery promise, revision policy, sizes — objection
  handling, not discovery copy.

## 6. Guardrails

- Tension without a real solution is clickbait; we always resolve on a
  concrete mechanism we actually deliver (tool, preview, revision, timeline).
- Exclusion is only used where it's true (min qty, teams).
- Survival-lever openings must describe problems our buyer genuinely has —
  deadlines, sizing, budget, blame — never invented catastrophes.
