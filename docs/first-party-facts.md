## The timeline (answers D1–D6)

- **Planning/decision phase: 2–3 weeks on average** from "this is our
  design" to confirmed order — for teams that have the time. Teams that
  start late compress it by necessity.
- **What the 2–3 weeks actually contains** (in observed order of drag):
  1. The design back-and-forth — when many options are shown and everyone
     votes, a natural loop.
  2. **Sponsor confirmation — the biggest single drag.** Sponsors want to
     see mockups; often request changes to logo visibility or positioning.
  3. Final revisions.
  4. **Money collection** — someone is always late paying the captain (or
     whoever places the order).
- **Size collection: usually under 24 hours.** NOT a bottleneck — by that
  stage everyone is excited that things are concluding. (This contradicts
  the industry's default assumption and our own earlier draft — it is a
  publishable, counterintuitive first-party finding.)
- **Decision:production ratio:** deciding (2–3 weeks) takes roughly one and
  a half to two times as long as production (7–12 days).
- **On-time record (D3): more than 95% of orders arrive within the official
  25–30-day door-to-door window.** Exceptions cluster around global
  logistics shocks (e.g. the start of the Iran war) and peak-season
  production capacity.
- **Fast lane: +€59** — skips the production queue and gets shipping
  priority; saves roughly 7 days.

## Process facts (reassurance / differentiation)

- **Every logo uploaded to the 3D designer is cleaned by a designer** before
  production — a message we actively convey to customers.
- **Bespoke orders are production-ready at confirmation** (the files were
  built during the design process); 3D-tool orders get a designer revision
  pass after ordering.
- 3D configurators give freedom but ask for the customer's time — and
  players do not always want to spend it. This is the honest limit of
  self-serve, and the reason curation matters.

## The Ready to Play origin story (editorial gold — clubs namable in blog only)

Watching the bespoke/AI briefs revealed patterns: **when Barcelona,
Juventus or AC Milan release a new kit, the references teams send us shift
within weeks.** So we built Ready to Play: our own curated,
studio-developed designs that allow full 3D customization — or just colours
+ logo for something that immediately looks pro. The collection updates
regularly, driven by our own demand data and by trends we catch in official
club releases.

- Concrete example: **the Brazil away kit 2026 motivated THE PHANTOM**
  (abstract camo). *(Editorial surfaces only — never on the product page.)*
- **Most popular model to date: THE FRACTURE.**
- RTP models sometimes get ultra-customized (extra patterns added) — the
  foundation is simply more developed than an empty template.

## Demand patterns

- **Bespoke/AI-to-real is drawing collectors and players, not only clubs**:
  fantasy-league players wanting to wear their own kits; solo orders;
  groups of friends who play FIFA together ordering their squad's kit.
- **US: growing organically since we began using US vocabulary; the 2026
  World Cup lines up with that growth.** (For the US piece — surface first,
